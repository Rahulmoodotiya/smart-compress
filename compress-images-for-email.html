Compress Images for Email

Large photo attachments may take longer to send and download or exceed an email provider's attachment limit. Compressing and resizing photos before attaching them can make email easier to share.

Resize before sending

If a photo will only be viewed on a screen, you may not need its full camera resolution. Resize to a sensible display dimension while keeping the aspect ratio to avoid stretching.

Which format should I use?

JPG is a dependable choice for photographs and widely supported by email clients. PNG is useful for screenshots, diagrams and transparent images. WebP can be smaller, but check that your recipient or workflow supports it.

Quality tips

Try a medium-to-high JPG quality setting and compare the result. For screenshots containing small text, PNG may preserve clarity better. Avoid repeatedly recompressing the same JPG because each lossy export can reduce quality.

How to reduce an attachment

Choose your photo in SmartCompress, select JPG for photos, adjust quality, optionally resize, then compress and download. Check the resulting file size before attaching it. You can process several images together, up to the tool's batch limit.

Privacy

SmartCompress processes image data in your browser. Your selected photos are not sent to an image-compression upload service by this workflow.
